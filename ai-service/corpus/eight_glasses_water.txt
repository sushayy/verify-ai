The advice to drink eight glasses of water a day is widely repeated but is
not grounded in a specific clinical finding. Hydration requirements vary
substantially with body size, activity level, climate, and diet, and health
authorities generally express intake guidance as a range rather than a fixed
number. Crucially, a significant portion of daily fluid intake comes from
food and from other beverages including tea, coffee, and juice, which the
eight-glasses framing typically ignores. The figure is often traced to a 1945
recommendation that did state a total fluid quantity but also noted that much
of it is present in prepared foods, a qualification usually dropped when the
advice is repeated. Drinking water is beneficial, but the specific number is
not an established medical threshold.
