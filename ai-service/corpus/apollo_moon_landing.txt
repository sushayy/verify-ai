Apollo 11 landed on the Moon on 20 July 1969, and Neil Armstrong and Buzz
Aldrin became the first humans to walk on its surface while Michael Collins
remained in lunar orbit aboard the command module. Six Apollo missions landed
crews on the Moon between 1969 and 1972, returning approximately 382
kilograms of lunar rock and soil that has since been studied by laboratories
worldwide. Retroreflectors left by the missions are still used today to
measure the Earth-Moon distance by laser ranging, an independent physical
confirmation of the landings. Twelve people in total have walked on the Moon.
