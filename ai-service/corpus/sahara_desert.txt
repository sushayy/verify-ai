The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world, covering approximately
9.2 million square kilometres across North Africa, an area comparable to that
of the United States or China. It spans eleven countries including Algeria,
Chad, Egypt, Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Morocco, Niger, and Sudan. It is not
the largest desert overall, since deserts are defined by precipitation rather
than temperature and both Antarctica and the Arctic are larger cold deserts.
Contrary to the common image of unbroken dunes, sand seas cover only about a
quarter of the Sahara's surface, with the remainder consisting of gravel
plains, rocky plateaus, and mountains.
