Evidence on vitamin C and the common cold is more limited than popular
claims suggest. Systematic reviews of controlled trials find that routine
vitamin C supplementation does not reduce the likelihood of catching a cold
in the general population. The same reviews do find a small but consistent
reduction in the duration of colds, on the order of eight percent in adults,
among people taking it regularly before symptoms begin. Starting vitamin C
after symptoms appear has not been shown to help. One exception appears in
people undergoing extreme physical stress, such as marathon runners and
soldiers in subarctic conditions, where trials have found a meaningful
reduction in cold incidence. Claims that vitamin C prevents colds outright
overstate what the trials show.
