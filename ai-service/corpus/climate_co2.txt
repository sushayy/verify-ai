Atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations have risen from roughly 280 parts
per million before the industrial revolution to above 420 parts per million
in recent years, a level unprecedented in at least the last 800,000 years
based on ice core records. The increase tracks closely with cumulative
emissions from fossil fuel combustion, cement production, and land use
change, and the isotopic signature of the added carbon identifies fossil
sources. Global average surface temperature has risen by approximately 1.1 to
1.2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. The scientific assessment
bodies of every major national academy of science concur that recent warming
is primarily driven by human greenhouse gas emissions.
