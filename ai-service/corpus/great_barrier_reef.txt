The Great Barrier Reef off the coast of Queensland, Australia extends for
approximately 2,300 kilometres and covers an area of around 344,000 square
kilometres, making it the world's largest coral reef system. It comprises
roughly 3,000 individual reefs and 900 islands and is visible from low Earth
orbit. The reef has experienced repeated mass coral bleaching events driven
by marine heatwaves, with significant events recorded in 1998, 2002, 2016,
2017, 2020, 2022, and 2024. Bleaching occurs when sustained elevated water
temperatures cause corals to expel the symbiotic algae they depend on for
most of their energy. Bleached coral is not necessarily dead and can recover
if temperatures fall soon enough.
