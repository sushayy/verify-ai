Which river is the world's longest is genuinely disputed rather than settled.
The Nile is conventionally listed at approximately 6,650 kilometres and the
Amazon at approximately 6,400 kilometres, which is the basis for the common
statement that the Nile is longest. However, the measurement depends on which
headwater tributary is treated as the source and how the river mouth is
defined, and several survey teams using different source points have argued
the Amazon is longer. The Amazon is unambiguously the largest river by
discharge volume, carrying roughly twenty percent of all freshwater entering
the oceans, far more than any other river. Statements that flatly declare
either river the longest omit that the ranking depends on contested
measurement choices.
