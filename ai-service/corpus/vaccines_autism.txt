The claim that vaccines cause autism originated with a 1998 paper that was
later retracted in full by The Lancet after investigators found the data had
been misrepresented. The paper's lead author was struck off the UK medical
register. Since then, large population studies covering millions of children
across multiple countries have found no association between vaccination and
autism. A Danish cohort study of more than 650,000 children published in 2019
found no increased autism risk following the measles, mumps, and rubella
vaccine, including among children considered at higher risk. Public health
agencies including the CDC and the World Health Organization state that the
evidence does not support any causal link.
