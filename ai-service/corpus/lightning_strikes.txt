The saying that lightning never strikes the same place twice is false.
Lightning preferentially strikes tall, isolated, conductive structures, which
means the same locations are hit repeatedly. The Empire State Building is
struck roughly twenty to twenty-five times per year and has been struck
multiple times during a single storm. Meteorological agencies note that tall
towers, radio masts, and isolated trees are all repeat targets, and lightning
research facilities deliberately exploit this by placing instrumented towers
at known strike-prone sites. A US park ranger was documented as having
survived being struck seven separate times over his career.
