The idea that humans use only ten percent of their brains is a myth with no
basis in neuroscience. Functional imaging studies using fMRI and PET show
activity across virtually the entire brain over the course of a day, and even
during sleep most regions remain metabolically active. The brain accounts for
roughly two percent of body mass but consumes about twenty percent of the
body's energy, which would be evolutionarily implausible if ninety percent of
it were idle. Damage to almost any brain region produces measurable deficits,
indicating there is no large reserve of unused tissue. The myth's origins are
unclear but it has been widely repeated in advertising and popular culture.
